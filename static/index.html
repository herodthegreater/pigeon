▸ Principles of carving
  1. Precision is cheap, structure is a cliff. Dropping bits degrades gently; removing whole channels can kill the model in one cut — so pruning always needs regrowth.
  2. Cut where the FLOPs are. A handful of stages hold most of the compute; trimming the tiny ones changes nothing.
  3. Cut where sensitivity is low. Scan first: some stages are redundant, some are load-bearing. Value = FLOP ÷ sensitivity.
  4. Cut a little, regrow, repeat. Small cuts + fine-tune (Recover) beat one big cut. Re-scan after each round — the map shifts.
  5. Trust FLOPs, not laptop latency. On a Mac the speed win hides; the FLOP/param drop is the portable prize for edge hardware.
