How to reverse-train a model: guide and glossary

What is reverse training?

Normal training grows a model from noise. Reverse training runs it backward: you start from a capable, fully-trained model and remove from it (precision, channels, whole layers) until only the one capability you need survives. The loop is always cut → measure → regrow → repeat.

The three levers

🎚️ Precision stores each weight in fewer bits. It shrinks the file size. Nearly free at 8-bit, it falls off a cliff around 4-bit unless you get clever about scales, level spacing and which stages you protect.

✂️ Structure removes whole channels. It's the only lever that makes the model faster, and it's a cliff: cut without regrowing and accuracy collapses. Cut by value.

🌱 Regrow fine-tunes after cutting. It heals pruning, and with the quantizer in the loop it teaches the model to live on a coarse bit grid. Training needs the Python server, so here it replays a recorded run.

Why does low precision break?

An N-bit weight can only take 2N−1 values (symmetric): 255 at 8-bit, 15 at 4-bit, just 3 (−s, 0, +s) at 2-bit. Every weight snaps to its nearest level. The step size is set by the largest weight in its scale group, so one outlier makes the steps coarse for everyone, and small weights round to zero. Open the microscope to watch it happen.

Glossary

Weight / parameter
a single number the model learned.
Channel / filter
a bundle of weights that detects one kind of feature; removing one shrinks the layer and speeds it up.
Stage (L0–L22)
a block of the network. L0 sees raw pixels; L22 is the detection head that writes boxes.
Quantization (bits)
fewer bits per weight (16→8→4…). Smaller file, same shape.
Scale
the step size of the bit grid. Weights sharing a scale share one ruler.
Granularity
who shares a scale: the whole tensor, each output channel, or small groups. Finer is more accurate, but each scale costs 16 bits of metadata.
Effective bits / weight
the real cost: bits per weight plus the scales spread over the weights. 4-bit in groups of 16 really costs 5.
INT vs FP levels
INT spaces levels evenly. FP (e.g. FP4 = E2M1) packs them near zero and spreads them at the tails, matching the bell shape of real weights. The idea behind NVFP4 and MXFP4.
Clipping
shrinking the range so the steps get finer, at the price of flattening outliers. It minimizes weight error, which isn't the same as keeping accuracy.
Mixed precision
different bits per stage: protect the fragile ones, crush the robust bulk.
SQNR
signal-to-quantization-noise ratio in dB: how loud the weights are compared with the rounding error. About 6 dB per bit.
Pruning
removing capacity. Structural means whole channels (a real speedup). Masking zeroes them as a live preview.
Sensitivity
how much mAP drops when you cut (or quantize) one stage. Low means redundant; high means load-bearing.
Value
payoff per unit of damage: FLOP (or weight) share ÷ sensitivity. Spend your cuts where value is high.
FLOPs
compute per image, the portable measure of speed.
mAP@50
detection accuracy: how well it finds birds (higher is better).
The cliff
the point where a smooth decline turns into collapse.
Fine-tune / regrow
retraining the wounded model to recover what a cut cost.
QAT
quantization-aware training: regrow while the forward pass uses the quantized weights, so the network adapts to them.

▸ Principles of reverse training
  1. Precision is cheap, structure is a cliff. Fewer bits degrade gently at first; removing channels can kill the model in one cut.
  2. Count effective bits, not nominal bits. Every scale is metadata. 4-bit in groups of 16 really costs 5 bits/weight.
  3. Put the levels where the weights are. Weights are bell-shaped: a float-like grid (FP4) beats an even grid (INT4) at the same size.
  4. Fragility isn't where the weight is. The first stages hold a few % of the weights but most of the precision sensitivity. Protect them cheaply; crush the bulk.
  5. Measure the task, not a proxy. Clipping minimizes weight error and can still lose accuracy. Only mAP counts.
  6. Cut where the FLOPs are, where sensitivity is low. Scan first: value = FLOP ÷ sensitivity.
  7. Cut a little, regrow, repeat. Regrowing heals cuts, and with the quantizer in the loop it heals precision too.
  8. Trust FLOPs, not laptop latency. The compute and size drop is the portable prize for edge hardware.
